# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

No single target segment is designed for specifically — the homepage is not written for investors, funders, or any one group. Anyone who lands on the site — a curious visitor, a potential shopkeeper partner, an end user, or an investor doing diligence — should be able to understand the studio and reach the product that matters to them.

Model: studio-first homepage, product-led routing. The homepage establishes what Purpose Here is and what it has actually built. Product pages (Bazaar, PurposePay) carry their own audience-specific messaging (e.g. Bazaar speaks to both shopkeepers and customers on its own page). The homepage does not try to pitch any one audience (explicitly not investors/funders) — credibility comes from the product being real and working, not from copy aimed at convincing a specific reader type.

## Product Purpose

Purpose Here is a one-person software studio run by Sachin, building real, working products rather than concepts. The website exists to show the studio's actual output honestly — what's live, what's in progress — and to route visitors to the specific product they care about.

Two products:
- **Bazaar: Nearby Shops** — hyperlocal shop discovery/ordering app for Tier-2/3 Indian markets. Status: live, onboarding phase. Tagline: "Bringing the market to your screen."
- **PurposePay** — personal finance tracker. Status: live web app, usable today in local mode; Google sign-in, UPI, and AI insights pending. Tagline: "Every rupee has a reason."

## Positioning

A solo developer shipping real, usable software — not a pitch deck, not a concept. The site's job is to be honest about what's live versus what's pending without undercutting itself. Traction and product quality do the convincing; the site does not need to argue a case for any specific audience.

## Operating Context

Built and maintained entirely from a phone (Termux, Acode, Expo Go — no laptop). Not a claim the site needs to foreground, but it explains why the studio is genuinely solo/scrappy rather than a positioning device.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Bazaar: working preview APK, direct download, Firebase Firestore backend live. No real shopkeepers onboarded yet as of last check — the site must not imply validated traction it doesn't have.
- PurposePay: live at purposepay.netlify.app, local-mode features (expense tracking, budgets, goals) work today; sign-in/UPI/AI insights are pending, not live.
- No testimonials, user counts, or traction claims exist — none should be fabricated or implied.
- Legal pages (privacy, terms) must be written generically/robustly enough to be linked from multiple surfaces (app store listings, both product pages, future surfaces) without rewriting per surface.

## Brand Commitments

- Existing tokens to evolve, not replace: primary orange `#E85D2C`, warm white/cream `#FCFAF6`, dark ink `#15130F`, gold accent `#FFB800`; Fraunces (serif, headings), Inter (body), JetBrains Mono (labels/tags).
- Text-only wordmark: "Purpose" + "Here" badge treatment, no icon-based logo yet.
- Voice: professional and human. Explicitly avoid AI-sounding or startup-cliché phrasing (e.g. "building in public," "early-stage journey" framing). Write like a person, not like generated copy.
- Social handle: @sachintiwari_here — visual identity should hold up consistently there too.

## Evidence on Hand

- Bazaar: screenshots (customer + vendor flows), working APK, Firestore live data.
- PurposePay: live deployed web app (local mode functional).
- No shopkeeper validation data, no user testimonials, no press — future work must not invent any of this.

## Product Principles

1. Show what's real before what's aspirational — no overstating status of either product.
2. One studio voice across both products; product pages specialize, homepage doesn't.
3. Do not design or write copy targeted at convincing investors/funders specifically — credibility comes from the product, not from persuasive framing.
4. Legal/policy content is written once, generically, for reuse across every surface it needs to be linked from.
5. Tone is professional and human — never reads as AI-generated or startup-buzzword-driven.

No product-specific requirement established yet.
